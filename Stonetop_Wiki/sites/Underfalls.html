Underfalls

A working Rime Lord shrine · hanging valley under the ice falls in the Whitefang Mountains, two days east of Barrier Pass · 10 nodes, one major arcanum

Codex · Rime Lords · Fae · Ice Sphere

Truth

Some things cannot be destroyed. The Hec’tumel Codex is one: copper does not burn, and the Pale Serpent only needs the next pair of hands. What cannot be unmade can still be stilled. The Rime Lords froze pockets of the world where nothing rots, nothing dreams, and nothing calls out; dark ice is a wall even a spirit cannot cross. People still carry great evils north and leave them here.

Underfalls is a working stasis-vault under a frozen waterfall — lamps lit, floors swept — tended without a break by the Vigil: nine mountain-folk who took the Rime Lord discipline and never left. The tulpas that guard it are dangerous the way a mill-wheel is dangerous: they are working.

Deep winter opens the shrine’s cistern cave as one of the mouths of the Bear of Winter’s domain. One of his retinue camps there every year the drifts hold: Hush-of-Snow. She waits at the only door that opens for her, charming to the nine keepers, ready for whoever climbs the stair with something they can no longer keep.

Underfalls takes deliveries — things too dangerous to keep and too durable to destroy. Some of it is the best hoard in the north, and none of it can be stolen: Fae cannot cross the vault threshold; a thing like this has to be given. Most winters nobody comes. When someone does, Hush is already at the door.

How she knows what’s coming answer this when they ask

She rarely knows a month ahead. Often she only knows a day out. Pick what fits the trail they left — and let them work out how she heard. That is the first real handle they get on her.

  • Carried unwrapped: the plates dream loudly, and everything with a nose for the arcane between here and Dhaar has been having the same dream. She followed the dream to the stair.
  • Word at Barrier Pass / Dhaar: a fortress-monastery is a noisy place, and Hush buys news the way other people buy bread (a song; a boy’s middle name). Somebody sold her the shape of travellers on the road.
  • The Vigil paid her news (without meaning to). They have been buying her politeness in small things since the first snow. One of those things was gossip. Dorji knows he did it (the Stilled Meadow).
  • Wrapped well, no word out: she simply is at her camp, as she is every winter — and works out what they carry from how they behave at the cistern. Her advantage is patience, not prophecy.

She never claims foresight. Asked directly she is delighted to explain, at length — every explanation is also an advertisement for what she can do for them.

“You can’t burn it, you can’t drown it, and you already know what happens to the one who keeps it. But there’s cold up north that stops things. Old cold, with people still minding it. Carry it up, ask them nicely, and walk home lighter.”

Three keys to the Gate of Quiet

Any one works.

  • The word Sthira — Hall of Chants, or study the Vault floor-runes (column-of-ice craft).
  • The Vigil’s token, a bronze tally given at the Cloister if they’re honest about what they carry.
  • Hush’s leave, bought at the Cistern. Cheapest at the table, most expensive later.
  • Or force it. The Warden is a made thing and can be broken. The Vigil will never forgive it.

Hush-of-Snow travels by snowdrift

She can show up at any room, once, whenever it would be interesting — sitting on the stair, walking the meadow, waiting in a room they already cleared. Use her early. She never strikes first. She only fights at the Vault, when the rite actually starts.

Before you leave

  • Cold kit: warm clothes, fuel, climbing rope. No fuel = every camp costs a provision or a debility.
  • Iron. Few pilgrims bring it to a holy mountain — and iron is the one thing Hush and her Billies genuinely fear (Fae lore). A plain iron nail is worth more up here than a sword.
  • Gifts. The Vigil takes no coin. They take salt, ink, lamp oil, and news. Bring some and the Cloister opens like a door.
  • Whatever they bury, wrap it. Unwrapped on the mountain, a living evil (Codex plates, a bound spirit, a curse that still speaks) gets a soft move each night — dreams, whispers, frost-writing.
  • Do not work the thing on the mountain. Casting from the Codex (or anything similar) above Dhaar is a shout. The Vigil closes the shrine to them; Hush raises her price one step.

Fae prices & debts — run it like this

Pressure is obligation — promises and prices. Use HAGGLE whenever they dicker with Hush or her Billies.

  • Every favour she does them has a price, and she is happy to let them name it later. “Later” is how Fae eat.
  • Hush’s ladder. Each time they take her help, refuse her, or insult her, move one step down — and tell them where they are: (1) she is charmed → (2) she is owed → (3) she is impatient → (4) she is offended. At 4 she calls the Billies at the Vault, and she names one PC’s promise out loud in front of the Vigil.
  • Iron in hand changes the conversation — she will not be threatened, she will simply leave and raise the price. Iron in a wound is what ends her.
  • The pressure is debt, not curse or transformation. The worst thing that happens here is that someone agrees to something.

Hush-of-Snow the antagonist

Fae, large, magical, devious, hoarder · of the Bear of Winter’s retinue · Instinct: to be owed

A tall woman built out of drift-snow and good manners, in a coat of white goat-hide, who leaves no prints. Sometimes a white hind at the edge of sight. She is unfailingly warm, remembers everyone’s name and their children’s names, and is never in a hurry. She has been here since the first snow, and she is going home with something.

Hush-of-Snow Armor 4 (resilience, ice) · 0 vs iron · (reach, forceful, grabby) · weave snow and glamour · call the Billies · be somewhere else · never strikes first

Same tracker wherever she appears. She cannot cross the vault threshold (the Vault) — that is the whole shape of the climax.

What she offers (escalating)

  • First: passage, warmth, a guide through the shrine, the Gate opened. Small prices. Charming.
  • Then: she will take the Codex off their hands entirely and swear it will never be read again — which is true. She will not swear it will never be lent.
  • Last (her real ask, when she is out of patience or they refuse the Codex): a true name — not a nickname. Someone living that a PC loves or is bound to: child, spouse, parent, dog. She phrases it as a trinket for her hoard: “Only to keep. I won’t call them. I won’t hurt them.” What that means: Fae who hold a true name can find that person, speak into their dreams, bind a promise to them, or sell the name on. She may not use it today. She will own it forever. That is the price.

Travel — Chart a Course

Stonetop → Highway → Foothills → Barrier Pass (5 days) → Dhaar valley (2 days) → the falls. Run 2 stops. One hard choice each.

1. Barrier Pass

“The pass is a wall, and the wall has a gate that hasn’t opened for southerners in living memory. Above it, prayer-flags snap over cliff-houses, and something you can’t quite see keeps pace with you along the parapet.”
  • Ask the road to Underfalls: Parley. Tell the truth and they give the road, a warning (“the drifts are open this year — do not eat anything you did not carry up”), and a letter to the Vigil that makes the Cloister friendly on sight.
  • Lie: they give a road a day longer that comes out at the Ice Falls instead of the Stair.
  • Skip the Pass: no letter, no warning. The first person who offers them food up here is Hush.

2. Dhaar valley

“Terraced barley under snow and a dozen roofs. Every lintel has a nail hammered into it, cold iron, driven deep — and the newest one is very new.”
  • Ask about the nails: a white lady has been walking the valley since the first snow. She is polite. She has bought two things from them already: a song, and a boy’s middle name. Nobody will say which boy.
  • Ask for a guide: old Lhamo will walk them to the stair for lamp oil, and tell them the Vigil are nine and count everything.
  • Take iron from a lintel: the valley will let them, unhappily. It is the single best decision they can make and they will not know why for two days.

The Vigil’s Cloister

Talk / the token
“Cells around a swept square. Nine bowls on a shelf, nine cloaks on nine pegs, one of them very old and never taken down. A woman with a shaved head is scraping ice off a step that has no ice on it, and she does not stop when you come in.”

The Vigil — nine living disciples of the Rime Lord tradition (order, not a fortress). Master Dorji (blind, 80s), Tashi (the step-scraper, speaks for them), six adepts, one novice: Nobu, fourteen, desperate for news of anywhere else.

Adept (×6, only if it comes to that) Armor 1 · staff (close, forceful) · disarm, bind, carry out — they do not kill
Nobu novice · hesitates, balks, flinches · will absolutely follow them home

They often know what is being carried before anyone says it — Dorji has spirit-sight and no manners about it. They are not shocked. Things are brought here to be put down. There is a procedure, and the procedure is the point.

  • Ask properly (Parley, or the Barrier Pass letter): they give the bronze tally — the Gate token, Key 2 — and the shape of the rite. Gifts of salt, ink, or lamp oil make this automatic.
  • Ask what it costs: nothing. But one of them comes down with them and watches, and if the visitors does anything to the shrine, that witness is what the mountain remembers.
  • Warn them about the white lady: Dorji’s face changes for the first time. They have been feeding her at the cistern all winter because turning her away costs more. That is the first honest fear in the place.
  • Push past / draw steel: six adepts, staves, nets. They aim to disarm and carry out, not kill. Losing means waking up outside, unhurt, with the shrine shut. Winning means the shrine is open and every tulpa inside now treats them as vermin (Nodes 7, 9 and 10 hostile on sight).

The Ice Falls

Way in Climb / spirit
“The waterfall stopped mid-fall a long time ago — a curtain of green-black ice, and the daylight through it is the colour of a bruise. Behind it, a dry hollow, and the ice is carved with names, smallest at the bottom, layered deep.”

A cold spirit lives in the falls — patient, bitter, dislikes exuberance, and has opinions about everyone in this valley. It does not attack. It tells.

  • Climb the face: Defy Danger +STR/+DEX, or rope-work and a real plan. Miss = and dangling. This is the way in for anyone avoiding the front door.
  • Talk to the spirit (offer stillness — sit, shut up, wait): it will answer three things. It knows: the white lady sleeps in the cistern; the Vigil are frightened of her; the vault door does not open for Fae, and she knows it, and she is not trying to open it.
  • Be loud, chip the ice, take a souvenir: the spirit freezes their water, their food and one held weapon. Nothing is destroyed. Everything is inconvenient.

Exits

Down the goat-track east → Prayer-Flag Stair: “The flags come back into view below you, and the swept stair, and whoever is standing at the top of it.”
The sluice channel, behind the curtain → Hall of Chants: “A cut stone channel that once fed the shrine, dry now, sloping into the mountain — and out of it, warm as breath, a hundred voices holding one note.”
The plunge basin, below the ice → Cistern Cave: “Under the frozen fall the basin has gone hollow, and a drift has piled against a cave-mouth that is deeper than the mountain has any business being. There are goat-tracks going in. None coming out.”

Hall of Chants

Key 1 — the word
“A long hall, and the chant is in your chest before your ears catch up — a hundred voices, none of them here. The pillars are carved with mouths. Pass a mouth and its part of the chant gets louder.”

A thoughtform — not real, but real enough. No attack, no HP, no malice. It has been reciting the same instruction for centuries. The instruction contains the stilling word, Sthira (the same command-word craft as a column of ice).

Getting the word

  • 10+: they hold it and its use — spoken at a made thing, it stops it dead for one exchange. Also the line the Warden wants: “nothing out.”
  • 7–9: the word, but rough. First use each scene, roll — 4+ it takes.
  • 6−: they mishear a syllable. It will fail once, at the worst moment, and they will not know why.

Advantage for: spirit-sight, Rime Lord script, the Vigil’s teaching (the Cloister), or one PC who will sit down and listen for an hour. Smashing a mouth-pillar ends that voice permanently — the Vigil will hear the gap in the chant from the courtyard, and Master Dorji will weep, and that is worse than being attacked.

Exits

South, under the closed-eye lintel → Cloister: “Cold clean air, a swept square, and nine bowls on a shelf.”
Up the sluice channel → The Ice Falls: “The chant thins behind you and the roar that isn’t moving gets louder ahead, until you step out under a wall of standing water.”
The pushed-out wall-block, behind the last pillar → Apes’ Hoard: “A gap where a block was shoved out from the far side. White hair caught on the stone, and a smell like a wet dog that has been eating flowers.”
The Long Stair, end of the hall → Gate of Quiet: “Steps going down into a cold with teeth, and at the bottom, a blue light that does not flicker, and something seated in it that has not moved in your lifetime.”

The Gate of Quiet

Gate — three keys Fight if forced
“A gate of dark ice, and standing in it a shape made of cold — a tall seated figure that is not sitting on anything. It performs the same courtesy it always has, and it performs it now, for you.”

The Warden — tulpa, purpose protection, tethered to the gate, cannot leave it. Not mad, not corrupt: literal.

The Warden Armor 2 (0 while stilled) · cold (reach, forceful) · unmanifest and reform · never pursues

Its three questions

  1. “What do you carry?” — the truth, or it knows.
  2. “Who sent you?” — “no one” is acceptable. Naming the Vigil without the tally is not.
  3. “What will you take out?” — “nothing” is the answer it wants. See the Reliquary.
  • Key 1 — the word: Sthira stops it for an exchange; walk past. It remembers, and it will not help them in the Vault.
  • Key 2 — the bronze tally: show it and the questions are a formality. It opens the gate and names the vault’s rite in order, which is worth a whole roll later.
  • Key 3 — her leave: Hush walks them to the gate and says one word into it. It opens. The Warden does not like this and says so, in a voice like a door: “That is not yours to give.”
  • Force: it fights, reforming twice at 6 HP unless stilled. Breaking it means the gate cannot be shut behind them — and in the Vault, Hush’s Billies can come through.

Exits

Back up the Long Stair → Hall of Chants: “Up out of the teeth-cold into the warm hum, and the mouths take up their parts as you pass.”
Back up the siphon → Cistern Cave: “Black water, two lungfuls, and then firelight and goats and somebody who says ‘back so soon?’ without looking up.”

The Stilled Meadow

Judgement
“A meadow in high spring, under a mountain, in winter. Grass to the knee, rhododendron in flower, a bee stopped mid-air over a bloom. Nothing here has moved in living memory — except the old man sitting in it, who is blind, and who says your names in the order you came through the gate.”

Master Dorji is here (he came down ahead of them, and no, they will not learn how), or Tenzi, a shantyara, if they made an enemy of the Vigil. Either way: this is where somebody looks at them and decides.

Tenzi (only if the Vigil were wronged) Armor 2 · orichalcum sword w/ advantage (2 piercing) · counters what she sees coming

Dorji’s question is not whether they may bury it. It is what they promised the white lady on the way down, and whether they intend to keep it. He can hear a Fae bargain on a person the way you can smell smoke.

  • Tell him everything: he tells them the thing the Vigil has never said aloud — the Vigil have been paying Hush all winter in small things, and they are out of small things. If they bury the Codex and refuse her, she will be the shrine’s problem, not theirs, and Dorji is going to let that happen, and he wants them to know he chose it.
  • Lie about a bargain: he says nothing, and the meadow’s stillness stops applying to the liar — their breath fogs, alone in the whole valley, for the rest of the adventure. Everyone can see it.
  • Ask him to do the rite instead: he cannot. Eighty years of stillness means his will can’t grip a thing that wants. That is the whole reason a shrine full of masters needs three loud southerners.
  • Wronged the Vigil? Tenzi is here instead, and she is not interested in the Codex. She is interested in whether they leave.

The Vault of Quiet: burial + boss

Climax
“A round chamber walled in dark ice. In the floor a cyst of it stands open like a cupped hand, set in a ring of rune-etched stone — the same craft as those lonely meadow pillars further north. It is clean. It is swept. Somebody comes down here every week to sweep a room almost no one enters.”

This is a worked vault built on the same principle as a column of ice: speak the command word near the dark ice, and it answers — opens, expands, seals, traps even spirits. Here the word is Sthira (Hall of Chants, or a few hours studying the floor-runes if they have Rime Lord script). Nothing lives in the chamber. The fight comes to the threshold: Hush-of-Snow arrives the moment the rite begins — now the thing must be given or lost. She cannot cross into the chamber. Neither can the Billies.

Hush-of-Snow Armor 4 · 0 vs iron · (reach, grabby) · at the threshold only · glamour, snow-weave, call the Billies
Billy (threshold) Armor 4 · 0 vs iron · — only if the Gate of Quiet was broken, or her ladder is at 4
Billy (threshold)

The shape of the fight

One PC has to stay in the chamber doing the rite. Everyone else is at a doorway holding off something with Armor 4 that cannot come in but can reach in, grab, glamour, drag, and talk. She goes for the rite-worker every time, and she goes for their ears first.

  • Iron ends her. Armor 0 against it. A nail, a horseshoe, a lintel-spike from Dhaar, the one in the ape hoard. Without iron this is a grinding, losing fight and they should feel it by the second exchange.
  • She will not fight to the death. At 8 HP or less she stops, brushes herself off, and makes her last offer as though nothing happened.
  • She keeps every promise made to her — that is the trap. If a PC owes her, she spends it here: “you said you would hand me the next thing I asked for. I am asking.”
  • Nobody has to fight at all. If they simply give her the Codex, she takes it, thanks them warmly, and the drift closes. That is a real ending. It is also a major arcanum in the hands of the Bear’s retinue, and the Vigil will have to be told.

Burial steps (in order)

Same move as the outdoor discovery: when you speak the command word near the dark ice, it expands or yields. At Underfalls the word opens the floor-cyst, receives what is set in, then knits shut — stilling what cannot be destroyed.

  1. Open the cyst — speak Sthira at the floor-runes (or +INT/+WIS with the Vigil’s rite / the Warden’s recital if they only half-know the word).
    • 10+: the ice cups open.
    • 7–9: opens; pick 1 — cold (ignores armor) · Hush gets a free reach-in now · the word is spent for this scene.
    • 6−: the floor doesn’t answer. No opening this beat, and she starts talking.
  2. Unwrap and set the Codex in — hands on it, +CON or Defy Danger. This is where the plates speak. Trigger Hec’tumel’s offer now if it hasn’t landed.
    • 10+: plates in, face down.
    • 7–9: in — pick 1: the bearer marks a Codex consequence · Hush has them by the wrist · someone else must pull them off it (an action).
    • 6−: the bearer doesn’t let go. Not dropped — held. Ask them why, out loud.
  3. Seal it — pack the cyst and speak the stilling over it: +INT, +WIS, or the dark ice knife driven into the seam.
    • 10+: the ice knits shut, purple-black, and the room goes quiet in a way they feel in their teeth.
    • 7–9: shuts, pick 1 — from the threshold · the knife is spent · a hand frozen into the seam (Defy +STR, damage and a debility).
    • 6−: the seam won’t take, and Hush names her price into the gap.
  4. Hold until it takes (only if she is still at the door) — +CON, or Defend the cyst one more exchange.
    • 10+: it locks. She stops mid-sentence, and the sentence never gets finished.
    • 7–9: locks; mark a debility or lose gear into the ice.
    • 6−: the seal sets crooked — buried, but she knows exactly where, and so does her aunt.

Hush at the threshold (dialog)

“Oh, don’t. Don’t put it in the ground. Something that old, and you’re going to bury it like a dog.”
“I can’t come in. You know that, I know that. So we can both stop pretending this is a fight and start talking about the price.”
(To whoever owes her) “You said yes to me at the cistern. In front of your friends. Are you a liar in front of them too?”
(At 8 HP or less, brushing snow off her sleeve) “Well. That was undignified. Last offer, and it’s a kind one.”

Hec’tumel’s offer use this

From the plates, to whoever is holding them — once, at step 2: “She will keep me on a shelf. You could keep me working. Refuse her and refuse them, and I will teach you, tonight, the word that closes a Fae’s mouth. You may bury me tomorrow. You will not want to.”
  • Accept: Hush is silenced and driven off outright. Mark a Codex consequence. The Codex is not buried; it is theirs now, with the Vigil watching them carry it back up the stair.
  • Refuse: the scene continues as it is. Dorji, upstairs, already knew which they’d pick and is unbearable about it.

If the Codex goes down

The vault seals purple-black and the mountain is quieter than any of them have heard anything be. Hec’tumel has no mouth in the world tonight, and whoever carried the Codex loses it — and any Codex spells with it, like a language you stop being able to hear.

  • The Sphere: Noruba’s Ice Sphere is theirs if the Vigil gave it or they took it. Cold, quiet, and a much politer road to the same appetite — deliberate.
  • Hush goes home owed nothing — a wound, for one of the Bear’s retinue. She knows the southern road they came by, and any promises spoken in her hearing. Winter is long.
  • The Vigil: the shrine took something in and the order held. They are nine, and may now count southern allies. An adept walks the road down before spring — a mountain trade partner if their home steading will believe it.
  • Nobu: if anyone was kind to him, he is two days behind them on the road, with a bowl and no cloak.
