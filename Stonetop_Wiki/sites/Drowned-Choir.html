The Drowned Choir

Suarachan lair · Ferrier’s Fen, ~2 days in from Marshedge · late autumn · a sunken Green Lord hall

Hunters · Singers · Soothsayer · Daagon

Truth

A Green Lord hall went down when the Makers collapsed this region. Something came to rest in the flooded bottom of it and dreams there — Daagon, Who Waits in Deep Waters, or something of its house. The suarachan sing it to sleep. They have sung for a very long time.

The singing is not a threat. It is the lid. The suarachan believe their songs keep the Things Below quiet, and they are right. They gather sacrifices in bulk in late autumn so the dark stays fed while they sleep through winter.

Isbeal — the Ferrier household girl handed over in place of a child of the blood — is here, alive. The Singers’ whole craft is keeping a prisoner living long enough. She has four or five days. So do three others taken from the fields.

The trap this site sets: the obvious heroic play is to kill the chorus and stop the singing. That is the worst outcome available. Do not warn them. Let the fiction warn them — the song falters, the water moves.

Salt the lever

Printed, and nobody thinks to bring it into a swamp: salt burns their skin — Hunters, Singers, and the Soothsayer alike.

  • Where they get it: a pouch of salt is commonly available in Marshedge at Value 1, no roll. A little box from the Hillfolk is Value 0 — they sell it for curing meat and know nothing about suarachan. Only the fen-walkers know what it does, and only if asked.
  • If they already have some: salt bought on the road for the trip is luck, not foresight. Play it as luck. Let them work out what it does down here.
  • Thrown in the face: damage, ignores armor, and drives them back a step (forceful).
  • A line laid across a threshold: Singers will not cross it. Hunters will, swearing, and take the d4 doing it.
  • A pouch is ◇ and holds about six handfuls. Track them. Running out at the wrong moment is the best thing that can happen to this session.

If nobody brought any: Fiadh has one pouch and will say so only when it is nearly too late — he is careful about what he admits to knowing.

The lullaby custom move

Audible from the Swallowed Stair down. It is not frogs. Give it to the players in writing when they first hear it.

When the chorus takes up the lullaby and you try to hold on to your own thoughts, roll +WIS: on a 10+, you hold; on a 7–9, pick 1 — mark dazed · you lose the thread and act last in the exchange · you find yourself humming, and the nearest Singer turns its head toward you; on a 6−, the GM says what happens — you drift, you kneel, you set down what you were carrying.
  • While the song is up: nobody hears a shout. No Aid at range, no warnings called, disadvantage to notice anything by ear.
  • Wax, wool, or stopped ears: skip the move, but you also cannot hear each other. Fine trade. Let them make it.
  • Trigger it once per area below, not per exchange.

Fiadh’s blight countdown

Fiadh — fen-wise, cautious, stealthy, observant; curious, kind, fen-blighted; Instinct to hide his affliction. HP 8; Armor 1; knife.

He is also the man who walked Isbeal out to the poles and did not look back. Deeper in, it shows:

  1. Poles: he will not look at them. Won’t say why.
  2. Stair: he goes into the black water without hesitating, and stays under too long.
  3. Chorus: the Singers do not attack him. They make room. He notices them noticing.
  4. Larder: he can’t meet Isbeal’s eyes. If pressed here, he says what he did.
  5. Mouth: the Soothsayer speaks to him as kin, by a name he has not used.

Don’t resolve this. He is not a traitor and there is no reveal — he is a decent man a few years further down a road, and whether the party is kind to him is the whole point.

Travel — Chart a Course

Drawn up in Marshedge, not Stonetop. Two days in, guide required.

Ferrier’s Fen sets its own terms. Choose 1:

  • A pouch of ◇ bendis root per day, burnt constantly (Value 1 each in Marshedge), or
  • The way is perilous, filled with dangers.

Then all of these:

  • You need a knowledgeable guide — a fen-walker.
  • The terrain is treacherous; you risk injury.
  • You risk contracting fen blight.
  • You risk drawing the attention of the suarachan. (Which is one way in — see the Poles.)
“Two days of mud that grabs and lets go, grabs and lets go. On the second afternoon the frogs stop — all of them, at once — and after that there is only one sound out there, and it is coming from under the water.”

1 · The Offering-Poles

Sign / choice Hunters watching
“A mudflat under three grand willows, and standing out of the muck a dozen peeled poles bound with woven grass. Some bindings are grey and rotted through. Two are new. The new ones have been cut open from the inside, or something was cut down off them.”

This is where the tithe is left. Isbeal was tied here nine days ago, and nothing came for her from the willows — so the suarachan took her instead.

Reading it

  • Track / Know Things / forest eyes: 10+ — a woman, boots, tied then carried east, and the carriers went on all fours part of the way. Also: the grey bindings go back decades, on a seven-year rhythm. 7–9 — the direction, not the pattern.
  • 6−: they’ve been watched the whole time. Hunters open with blowguns from the reeds.
  • A scrap of Ferrier household cloth is on the newest pole if anyone searches. Fiadh identifies it without being asked, then wishes he hadn’t.

Hunters in the reeds

4 Suarachan Hunters · group, stealthy, cautious, organized, amphibious · Armor 1 (scaly hide) · cudgel (close, crude) · tongue (reach) · blowgun (near) · night vision · salt burns · Instinct to bring home captives

They do not open with violence unless spotted-and-startled or the party rolled a 6− above. They want captives, not corpses, and they are patient. The needles are poison — a hit means Defy Danger +CON or go heavy-limbed (disadvantage) for the fight.

  • Fight them: straightforward. Salt ends it fast. Survivors flee east and the hall knows they’re coming.
  • Follow them home: best play. Defy +DEX to trail them through the reeds; arrives at the Stair unnoticed and skips the Stair’s first hazard.
  • Let themselves be taken: genuinely viable and the suarachan will oblige — it is their Instinct. Bound, disarmed (gear goes to the Larder’s pile), carried straight to area 4. Cuts areas 2 and 3 on the way in and makes them the escape route instead.
“East, where the willows thin, the water goes from brown to black in a line as straight as a wall. Something under it is holding a shape that the Fen never made.”

2 · The Swallowed Stair

Descent Hazard
“A Maker-cut stair goes down into the black water at a wrong angle, the whole hall tipped like a cart in a ditch. Green Lord work — the stone is grown, not laid, and there are faces in it that turn away when you look. Forty feet down the steps, the singing starts.”

Three ways down. Each costs something different. First mention of the lullaby.

Ways down

  • Wade the stair. Shallowest, slowest. Snaplilies carpet the flooded steps: 1d4 (hand, grabby) as they latch on, and anyone who trips among them takes with advantage (grabby, messy). Move slow and calm = no roll. Rush or panic = Defy +DEX.
  • Rope down the light-shaft. A cracked vent above the hall. Fast and dry, but you come out over the Chorus and the rope scrapes — Defy +DEX or arrive with fifty faces already turned up at you.
  • The culvert. A flooded Green Lord drain, forty feet underwater in the dark. Defy +CON. Miss = surface in area 3 out of breath and separated from the others. Comes out past the Chorus entirely on a success.
First lullaby roll happens here, on the stair, before anyone has to fight anything. Let them learn what the song does while it is still cheap.
“The stair opens out. You are standing on a gallery above a hall the size of the whole village, half full of still black water, and every ledge of it is crowded.”

3 · The Chorus

Crossing Do not silence it
“Hundreds of them. Squatting on every ledge and stair and windowsill of a drowned hall, throats working, eyes shut. Not one of them looks at you. The sound is so deep and so loud you feel it in your teeth and the water in front of you shivers with it.”

This is a room to cross, not a fight to win. They are a horde of hundreds and they are busy. They will not break off the song for trespassers unless the song is threatened.

Singers

Suarachan Singers · horde, amphibious · HP 3 · Armor 0 · fists and bite w/ disadvantage (hand) · tongue w/ disadvantage (reach) · night vision · salt burns · mutations · Instinct to sing the eternal lullaby

Don’t track individuals. Track how much of the song is still going.

Crossing it

  • Walk through quietly: Defy +DEX as a group, or one roll with Aid. Success — across, untouched, deeply unsettled. Miss — a knot of a dozen rouses and swarms; salt or a hard shove breaks them, and the song thins where they broke off.
  • Salt line behind you: covers the retreat later. Costs a handful. Worth it.
  • Ask Fiadh: he knows the ledge route. He also knows they won’t touch him, and would rather the party didn’t find that out.

If they attack the chorus

Easy kills — HP 3, Armor 0, attacks at disadvantage. Let them feel good about it for one exchange. Then, per exchange the song is broken:

  1. The water stops shivering. It goes flat, and then it goes taut.
  2. Every Singer still alive stops singing at once and looks at the water. Not at the party.
  3. Something very large turns over below the floor. Lights go out. Everyone marks dazed.
  4. The Soothsayer comes up from area 5 alone, unarmed, and begs — this is the only time she begs.
  5. Impending doom: it wakes. End the session on that if you have to; do not fight it.
Anyone who gets the song going again — Singers left alive, the Soothsayer, a PC who hums along and means it — resets this to 1. That last one is a real option and a real cost.
“At the far end, above the waterline, the hall’s old gallery has been stuffed with woven grass — baskets the size of a man, hanging in rows. Some of them are moving.”

4 · The Larder

The prize Choice
“Woven grass cages, hung from Green Lord stonework, above the waterline where things keep longest. Four hold people. Two hold what’s left of people. There is a pile of taken gear in the corner, sorted — boots with boots, knives with knives — by something that has watched humans a long time and never understood them.”

Who is here

  • Isbeal — Billy’s. Ferrier household, handed over in place of a child of the blood. Nine days in a cage. Lucid, furious, and will not be carried; she walks out or nobody does. She knows exactly which four men of the Council put her on that pole, by name.
  • Two field-hands from the Marshedge fields — taken in the autumn sweep. One can walk. One cannot.
  • A fourth, months gone, well down the fen blight track and hiding his hands. He begs to come. Taking him home means taking the blight to Marshedge, and Fiadh knows it, and Fiadh will not be the one to say so.

The choice is carrying capacity, not morality: quiet and fast with one, slow and loud with four. Don’t editorialise. Let them count.

Hunters return

3 Hunters come to check the stock — automatically, a few minutes after the party arrives. They raise a shrill alarm before closing.

  • Let one flee: the Soothsayer comes up. Area 5 happens here instead, on her terms.
  • Gear pile: everything taken off every captive for years. One good find, GM’s pick — a Value 1–2 item, or a Green Lord thing that came up out of the deep hall and was sorted in with the knives because it is shaped like one.
“Below the cages the floor opens — no stair, just a hole where the hall’s bottom gave way, and the water in it is going down and down. The singing is louder pointed at that hole than it is anywhere else. They are all singing at it.”

5 · The Dreaming Mouth

Climax Optional
“The bottom of the hall is gone. Below is water so still it looks like floor, and under that, a long way under, something pale turns over slowly the way a sleeper turns. It is bigger than the hall. The hall is inside it, or was.”

Daagon, or something of its house. Not a fight. Not ever a fight. It is scenery with a pulse, and its Instinct is to take from the surface and never let go.

They do not have to come here. If they took the quiet exit from the Larder, they never see it — and that is a fine, and slightly better, version of this session.

The Soothsayer

Suarachan Soothsayer · solitary, corrupted, cunning, magical, amphibious · Armor 0 · poisonous skin (hand) · tongue (reach) · night vision · salt burns · marked by the Things Below · Instinct to mollify the Things Below

The Soothsayer secretes poisons: debilitating, hallucinogenic, deadly · uses a gift of the Things Below

Bright wet scales, slick with slime, and she has been eating her own visions for so long she is barely in the room. She is not guarding a hoard. She is holding a lid down, and she has been doing it since before Marshedge was built.

She can be talked to

Suarachan have no language — true communication needs a soothsayer’s touch, which means her poison. When a PC accepts it: they perceive the spirits here and can speak with any that are active, including her, including the thing below if they are stupid enough to address it. When it wears off, mark a debility (dazed if available).

“You take the meat. Fine. Meat is nothing. The song is the thing.”
“Your people leave them on the poles. We only carry them in out of the rain.”
“Winter comes. We sleep. It does not. Something must be in its mouth while we sleep.”
“Take four and leave us nothing, and we will come to your fields for four more. That is not a threat. That is arithmetic.”
(to Fiadh, in a voice he understands) “Brother. Your hands. How long now?”

Ways this ends

  • Walk out with the captives while the song goes on. The intended ending. Nothing is cleansed, nothing is killed, the lullaby continues, and Isbeal goes home. This is a win — say so at the table, in those words.
  • Kill her. HP 12, Armor 0 — easy, and the poison skin means whoever does it in melee eats a d10. Then the chorus has no soothsayer, and the countdown in area 3 starts on its own over the following weeks. Seed for later.
  • Bargain. She wants the mouth fed through winter. Offer her something else that will do — livestock, the drowned dead of the Fen, a promise about the poles, the four Council names Isbeal is carrying. Ferrier’s Fen runs on prices; hold her to a real one.
  • Wake it. If they broke the chorus in area 3 and came down here anyway, the water rises to meet them. Run it as escape, not combat. Nobody rolls to hurt it.

Getting out

  • Quiet exit (no alarm, chorus intact): back through the Chorus and up the stair. One lullaby roll. Done.
  • Loud exit (alarm raised, Soothsayer dead, or captives slowing them): Hunters pursue through the reeds for two days. Two hard moves on the way out — separate them in the dark, drown a light, take back a captive. A salt line laid earlier cancels one.
  • Carrying more than one: travel is slow. Add a day, and everyone rolls to resist fen blight who spent time in the black water.

Aftermath

  • Billy builds the mill. He is the exceptional engineer/foreman the Mill requires, and this was his price. Follower, or he comes to Stonetop with Isbeal — his call, not the party’s.
  • Isbeal has four names. She was on that pole because Marshedge substituted her for a Ferrier child of the blood, and she knows who signed off. What the party does with that is the Marshedge thread for next session.
  • The debt is still open. The Willow Witches were owed a child and were paid a servant. That price was not accepted. Nobody in Marshedge wants to think about it.
  • Fiadh goes home a little further along than he left. If the party was decent to him, he can be Recruited later — Cost: secrets of the Fen.
  • If the chorus was thinned: start a slow countdown. Late winter, the frogs stop again, and it is heard from the Marshedge palisade.
