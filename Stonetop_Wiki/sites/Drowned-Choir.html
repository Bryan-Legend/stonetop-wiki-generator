The Drowned Choir

Suarachan lair · Ferrier’s Fen, ~2 days in from Marshedge · late autumn · a sunken Green Lord hall

Hunters · Singers · Soothsayer · Daagon

Truth

A Green Lord hall went down in the Cataclysm. Daagon dreams in the flooded floor. The suarachan sing it to sleep. Late autumn they stock captives so it stays fed while they sleep through winter.

Isbeal is alive, in the Larder. Killing the chorus wakes what is under it.

Riona escorting

Riona — fen-walker, fen-wise, cautious, stealthy, observant; prepared, agile, stubborn; Instinct to assume the worst; Cost competence shown. HP 8; Armor 1 (leather cuirass); knife (hand) · sling (near, reload). Spot/avoid a hidden hazard · berate outsiders for carelessness · do something gross without flinching.

Riona Armor 1

The party’s guide. Treat her as a follower only if someone Recruited her; otherwise she takes her own cuts. She packed salt (six handfuls), wax for four, a rope, and bendis.

Salt the lever

Salt burns Suarachan skin

  • Thrown in the face: damage, ignores armor, and drives them back a step (forceful).
  • A line laid across a threshold: Singers will not cross it. Hunters will, swearing, and take the d4 doing it.
  • A pouch is ◇ and holds about six handfuls. Track them.

The guide has a pouch if nobody else brought one.

The lullaby custom move

The song starts at the Stair. It is not frogs. Write the words out when they first hear it.

When the chorus takes up the lullaby and you try to hold on to your own thoughts, roll +WIS: on a 10+, you hold; on a 7–9, pick 1 — mark dazed · you lose the thread and act last in the exchange · you find yourself humming, and the nearest Singer turns its head toward you; on a 6−, the GM says what happens — you drift, you kneel, you set down what you were carrying.
  • While the song is up: nobody hears a shout. No Aid at range, no warnings called, disadvantage to notice anything by ear.
  • Wax, wool, or stopped ears: skip the move, but you cannot hear each other.
  • Trigger it once per area below, not per exchange.

Travel — Chart a Course

Drawn up in Marshedge, not Stonetop. Two days in. A fen-walker is already along as guide.

Ferrier’s Fen sets its own terms. Choose 1:

  • A pouch of ◇ bendis root per day, burnt constantly (Value 1 each in Marshedge) — the guide brought it; they still pay, or
  • The way is perilous, filled with dangers.

Then all of these:

  • The terrain is treacherous; you risk injury.
  • You risk contracting fen blight.
  • You risk drawing the attention of the suarachan. (Which is one way in — see the Poles.)
“Two days of mud that grabs and lets go, grabs and lets go. On the second afternoon the frogs stop — all of them, at once — and after that there is only one sound out there, and it is coming from under the water.”
“Keep the root burning. You stop, the midges write their name on you.”
“She’s dead. We’re walking in for bones. Pack for that and be glad if I’m wrong.”

The Offering-Poles

Sign / choice Hunters watching
“A mudflat under three grand willows, and standing out of the muck a dozen peeled poles bound with woven grass. Some bindings are grey and rotted through. Two are new. The new ones have been cut open from the inside, or something was cut down off them.”

This is where the tithe is left. Isbeal was tied here nine days ago. The Witches did not take her; the suarachan did.

Reading it

Hunters in the reeds

They do not attack unless startled or the party rolled a 6− above. They want captives, not corpses. A blowgun hit means Defy +CON or go heavy-limbed (disadvantage) for the fight.

  • Fight them: salt ends it fast. Survivors flee east, and the hall knows they are coming.
  • Follow them: Defy +DEX through the reeds. They reach the Stair unnoticed and skip its first hazard.
  • Let themselves be taken: bound, disarmed, gear dumped on the Larder pile, carried straight there. The Stair, Chorus, and Light-well become the way out.
“East, where the willows thin, the water goes from brown to black in a line as straight as a wall. Something under it is holding a shape that the Fen never made.”
“I don’t look at them. That’s the rule. You look if you want.”
“Charter work. Ferriers’ year. They sent a servant. I walked her. I didn’t look back. That’s how it’s done.”

The Swallowed Stair

Descent Hazard
“A Maker-cut stair goes down into the black water at a wrong angle, the whole hall tipped like a cart in a ditch. Green Lord work — the stone is grown, not laid, and there are faces in it that turn away when you look. Forty feet down the steps, the singing starts.”

Three ways down. The first lullaby roll happens here.

Exits → Offering-Poles Reed-loft Light-well (shaft) The Chorus (wade) The Larder (culvert, dashed)

Ways down

  • Wade the stair → Chorus. Shallowest, slowest. Snaplilies carpet the flooded steps: 1d4 (hand, grabby) as they latch on, and anyone who trips among them takes with advantage (grabby, messy). Move slow and calm = no roll. Rush or panic = Defy +DEX.
  • Rope the light-shaft → Light-well. A cracked vent, fast and dry. No roll if they set the rope carefully. If they rush it, Defy +DEX or the rope scrapes and faces below turn up.
  • The culvert → Larder. A flooded Green Lord drain, forty feet underwater in the dark. Dashed on the map. Defy +CON. Success — past the Chorus entirely, surface at the cages. Miss = surface in the Chorus out of breath and separated.
“The stair opens out. You are standing on a gallery above a hall the size of the whole village, half full of still black water, and every ledge of it is crowded.”
“Don’t. Touch. The. Song.”

The Light-well

Overlook
“A dry gallery, Green Lord stone, a crack of grey fen-light in the roof. The hall falls away under you. Hundreds of throats working on every ledge. At the far end, cages. In the floor of the hall, a hole the singing is aimed at. The faces in the stone here turn away from that hole, and they keep turning if you watch.”

The whole site is visible from here. The ledge to the Larder skips the Chorus floor.

Exits → Swallowed Stair The Chorus (drop) The Larder (ledge, dashed)

  • Look: no roll. The Chorus is hundreds and busy. Four cages are moving, two are still. The song is aimed at a hole in the floor. East, a warmer jelly-pool — the Spawn-cistern.
  • The faces: 10+ — a Green Lord household, still turning from what came to rest in the floor. 7–9 — they turn from the hole, not from you. 6− — one looks at you, and for a breath you look at the hole.
  • Ledge to the Larder: Defy +DEX as a group. Miss: a scrape, Singers look up; drop, or spend a handful of salt and keep going.
  • Drop to the Chorus: fast and loud. Faces already turned.

A basin under the vent still holds black water, the same as the hole. Someone can take a vial.

“Now you look. Then we go around. We do not go through.”
“Cages are moving. I was wrong. That happens. Once.”

The Chorus

Crossing Do not silence it
“Hundreds of them. Squatting on every ledge and stair and windowsill of a drowned hall, throats working, eyes shut. Not one of them looks at you. The sound is so deep and so loud you feel it in your teeth and the water in front of you shivers with it.”

A horde of hundreds, and they are busy. They will not break the song for trespassers unless the song itself is threatened.

Exits → Swallowed Stair Light-well Spawn-cistern The Larder

Singers

Suarachan Singers · horde, amphibious · HP 3 · Armor 0 · fists and bite w/ disadvantage (hand) · tongue w/ disadvantage (reach) · night vision · salt burns · mutations · Instinct to sing the eternal lullaby

Don’t track individuals. Track how much of the song is still going.

Crossing it

  • Walk through quietly: Defy +DEX as a group, or one roll with Aid. Success: across, untouched. Miss: a dozen rouse; salt or a shove breaks them, and the song thins there.
  • Salt line behind you: covers the retreat later. Costs one handful.

If they attack the chorus

HP 3, Armor 0, attacks at disadvantage. Per exchange the song is broken:

  1. The water stops shivering. It goes flat, and then it goes taut.
  2. Every Singer still alive stops singing at once and looks at the water. Not at the party.
  3. Something very large turns over below the floor. Lights go out. Everyone marks dazed.
  4. The Soothsayer comes up from the Mouth, unarmed, and begs.
  5. It wakes. Run this as escape, not combat.

Restart the song (Singers left, the Soothsayer, or a PC who hums and means it) and this resets to 1.

“At the far end, above the waterline, the hall’s old gallery has been stuffed with woven grass — baskets the size of a man, hanging in rows. Some of them are moving. Off the east wall the water is warmer, and thicker.”

The Spawn-cistern

Side chamber Leave them
“A side pool off the hall, warmer than it has any right to be. The water is jelly-thick with egg-mass. Things the size of a fist turn over in it — too many eyes, too many limbs, some of them already humming, badly, the way a child hums a song it does not know yet. One Singer sits on the lip and does not look at you.”

Next year’s hunters. Harming them thins the song in the hall.

Exits → The Chorus Dreaming Mouth (flooded crack)

  • The tender: one Singer, HP 3, Armor 0. It will not attack. It alarms if the eggs are touched.
  • Harm them: each mass broken is one step on the Chorus countdown. The tender alarms. The Soothsayer comes up from the Mouth to see who did it.
  • The crack down: Defy +CON, or ride the water to the Mouth coughing. No Singer follows.
“Leave them. I have done ugly work. I will not watch you invent worse.”
“Those aren’t children. They’re next year’s hunters. You start here and the song breaks.”

The Larder

The prize Choice
“Woven grass cages, hung from Green Lord stonework, above the waterline where things keep longest. Four hold people. Two hold what’s left of people. There is a pile of taken gear in the corner, sorted — boots with boots, knives with knives — by something that has watched humans a long time and never understood them.”

Exits → The Chorus Light-well (ledge) Swallowed Stair (culvert) Dreaming Mouth

Who is here

  • Isbeal — Billy’s girl. Nine days in a cage. Lucid, furious; she walks out or nobody does. She knows four Council names.
  • Two field-hands from the Marshedge fields, taken in the autumn sweep. One can walk. One cannot.
  • A fourth, months gone, well down the fen blight and hiding his hands. He begs to come. Taking him home means taking the blight to Marshedge.

Quiet and fast with one person. Slow and loud with four.

“I walk. You don’t carry me.”
“Four names. I know who put me on that pole.”

The Soothsayer

Suarachan Soothsayer · solitary, corrupted, cunning, magical, amphibious · Armor 0 · poisonous skin (hand) · tongue (reach) · night vision · salt burns · marked by the Things Below · Instinct to mollify the Things Below

The Soothsayer secretes poisons: debilitating, hallucinogenic, deadly · uses a gift of the Things Below

Bright wet scales. She has been holding a lid down since before Marshedge was built.

Talk

Suarachan have no language. True speech is her poison. If a PC accepts it: they perceive the spirits here and can speak with her, and with the thing below. When it wears off, mark a debility (dazed if available).

“You take the meat. Fine. Meat is nothing. The song is the thing.”
“Your people leave them on the poles. We only carry them in out of the rain.”
“Winter comes. We sleep. It does not. Something must be in its mouth while we sleep.”
“Take four and leave us nothing, and we will come to your fields for four more. That is not a threat. That is arithmetic.”
(if they harmed the spawn) “Those were the next throats. You have made winter louder.”

Ways this ends

  • Walk out with the captives, song still going. Isbeal goes home. The lullaby continues.
  • Kill her. HP 12, Armor 0. Melee eats a d10 from the skin. Chorus countdown starts on its own over the following weeks.
  • Bargain. She wants the mouth fed through winter. Livestock, drowned dead, a promise about the poles, Isbeal’s four names.
  • Wake it. The water rises. Run it as escape, not combat. Nobody rolls to hurt it.

Getting out

  • Quiet exit (no alarm, chorus intact): ledge back to the Light-well, or through the Chorus and up the stair. One lullaby roll. Done. The loft climb is dry if they used it on the way in.
  • Loud exit (alarm raised, Soothsayer dead, spawn harmed, or captives slowing them): Hunters pursue through the reeds for two days. Two hard moves on the way out — separate them in the dark, drown a light, take back a captive. A salt line laid earlier cancels one.
  • Carrying more than one: travel is slow. Add a day, and everyone rolls to resist fen blight who spent time in the black water.

Aftermath

  • Billy is the exceptional engineer/foreman the mill needs, and this was his price. Follower, or he comes to Stonetop with Isbeal.
  • Isbeal has four Council names. She will use them.
  • The Witches’ debt is still open: they were owed a child and were paid a servant. That price was not accepted.
  • If the chorus was thinned or the spawn harmed: start a slow countdown. Late winter the frogs stop again, heard from the palisade.
