The Quern

A prophecy plotline · Stonetop to Gordin’s Delve · a titan pinned under the Third Delve, and the Stone is its last nail

The Cistern · The Stone · The Delves · Labyrinth · West Road · Tor · Ustrina

Truth

A primordial titan lies pinned under the Huffel Peaks. Tor is Slayer-of-Beasts, and the village tale says he slew three; the skeleton at Titan Bones is one. The Quern is the fourth, the one he could not kill. He put a javelin through its back and the giants did the rest. Forge Lord plaques call it Abaloura, “the tremor”. The village calls it the Quern because it turns under the hill and grinds, in a village that has no mill.

The binding is the one work the Makers shared besides the roads. The Forge Lords cast seven orichalcum pins and drove six through the beast. The Stone Lords sang the binding into the bedrock along a line from the Peaks to the bluff, and the West Road is the visible spine of it. The seventh pin is the Stone: Tor’s javelin, fifty feet showing. Lightning striking it keeps the binding charged, which is why storms come to it and why the village air tingles of ozone.

The Cistern is the binding’s ear and drum, a Stone Lord vault with a floor of makerglass that carries the Stone’s ringing into the rock. The water is the medium. The villagers have filled the Makers’ tuning chamber with snowmelt for twelve generations, which is why nobody in Stonetop is bothered by loud noises and everyone talks a little too loud.

Daagon has a Mouth in the lake at the bottom of the Second Delve and has whispered to the Quern through the water for centuries: they are pulling your nails; the last one is on the hill; go and break it. The nails are what Gordin’s Delve calls the orichalcum “doors” in the Third Delve (Delve secrets). Miners pry one open every few years and a quake follows. Four of seven are out.

Mutra the Teeth wants the last two because Ustrina pay silver for their masters’ work. His powder-woman Gessa Blackpalm wants them because Daagon told her where they are. When the sixth comes out the Quern can move, and the only way it can go is along the binding under the road, three days toward the nail it can reach from beneath. It surfaces under the bluff, breaks the Stone, and Stonetop goes down the crack.

“Rings spread across the Cistern water, and nothing has been dropped in. The dogs will not come near the mouth. Somewhere under your feet, something very large has shifted its weight.”

Prophecy

Every child in Stonetop knows the skipping rhyme. “Horn” is the Cistern and “anvil” is the Stone, Tor’s drinking horn and Tor’s anvil (Tor in Stonetop).

Quern’s asleep and the anvil’s ringing,
count the thunder, the Thunderhead’s singing.
Horn runs cold and the horn runs still —
good for the barley, good for the kill.
Horn runs warm and the horn runs humming —
shut the door, the Quern is coming.
Horn runs black and the anvil’s broke —
that’s the year of the last of the folk.

Twymor has a longer stanza, copied from a Marshedge antiquarian’s book of Barrow Builder verse. Nobody sings this part.

Seven nails for the beast Tor could not kill,
six in the mountain and one in the hill.
Draw them out and the road-stones sweat;
draw the last and the horn runs wet.
The road runs both ways, the chain runs one —
what climbs the road comes for the Stone.
  • “Shut the door” is the answer: the doors are the nails.
  • “Draw the last” is the line Gessa’s cult reads as an instruction.
  • “The horn runs wet” is the line that gets the village arguing to drain the Cistern.
  • Fresh delivery: the first violent storm of summer, lightning walks the Stone all night and half the village dreams the same beast, chained on its back under a mountain, each dreamer one link (Tor hook). Everyone wakes humming the rhyme. Beth says the Cistern was doing it first.

Portents

Advance one when Seasons Change says a threat gets worse, when a pin comes out, or when the party spends a week elsewhere. Each pin driven home reverses one. A storm with the Stone charged and the binding re-tuned puts the Quern back to sleep where it lies.

  1. The horn rings. Rings on the Cistern water with nothing dropped in. Only children and dogs hear the hum. Goats will not drink from it. The ozone smell is stronger at the Cistern mouth than at the Stone. This is already happening.
  2. The storm dream. The whole village dreams the chained beast. Erin the Legend’s statue cracks across the base. Three days later a tremor reaches Stonetop: pin five is out.
  3. The horn runs warm. Cistern water warm at dawn, tasting of iron. The Quiet Twins stand at the foot of the bluff pointing up at the village. A sinkhole in a fallow field shows a broken road-stone with scorched runes. Yohan’s cult keeps vigil at the Cistern at night.
  4. The road-stones sweat. Pin six is out. A real quake: the Ringwall splits, the southeast watchtower comes down, the West Road’s pavestones run with black water. That night a Hand of Daagon rises through the Cistern floor (the Cistern).
  5. The anvil rings. Lightning strikes the Stone out of a clear sky, night after night. Everyone hears the hum now. Livestock bolt down the switchback. The Cistern drains itself into a crack that was not there yesterday. Stones of the Old Wall tumble in a line from the end of the road toward the bluff.
  6. Impending doom. At the next storm the Quern surfaces beneath the bluff and breaks the Stone (the Stone). The bluff collapses into the Cistern. Stonetop is a crater.

Site moves

  • When the Quern turns (a pin pulled, a portent advanced), everyone in Stonetop rolls +nothing. 10+ you keep your feet and see what falls; 7–9 the tremor reaches a thing of yours (a wall, a still, a fence, a sleeping child’s cot); 6− the ground opens somewhere in the village and something is lost into it. Earthquake hazards are on Huffel Peaks page.
  • When you put your hand flat on the Cistern water and listen, roll +WIS. 10+ you learn where the Quern is on the line and how many nails still hold; 7–9 you learn one of those, and the water tells Daagon where you are; 6− you hear the whisper and it can now compel you (whispers and visions).
  • When you drive a pin home in the beast’s back, roll +STR, with advantage if an Ustrina alloyed it and a Stonetop smith set it. 10+ it seats and the Quern sleeps deeper (reverse one portent); 7–9 it seats, but the beast turns and the tunnel comes down behind you; 6− the pin flares and slags, and the Quern knows where you are.

The Cistern

Stonetop Hand of Daagon
“The water is black and still, and then it is not. Rings walk out from the middle to the walls and back. The air tastes of a storm that is not here. Beth is sitting on the rim with her feet over the edge.”

A Maker vault under the village, filled with rain, snow and Stream water. Use the table’s answers for the opening and whether anyone has been down; the default is a stone kerb over a shaft, a ladder, and a floor of makerglass under twenty feet of water. Children play here. Folks gossip here.

  • Listen: the site move (hand on the water). Yohan’s cultists have been doing it every night, and two of them can now be compelled.
  • Dive to the floor: Defy Danger +CON for the breath. The floor is a single disc of glass, warm, and it hums against the palm. Runes ring its edge in the same hand as the Stone. Know Things here is how the party learns what the Cistern is.
  • Gwenda’s rite: bread and whisky on the water at dusk. The rings stop for a night. Daagon answers her the next morning in her sleep, and the answer is a life. Robert volunteers. If the village does it, the Cistern goes quiet, the Quern stops for good, and the water is Daagon’s forever: a Mouth under Stonetop.
  • Quinton’s drain: after the Hand, he wants the water out and the shaft bricked. Drained, the binding goes silent and the Quern moves faster; advance a portent. Persuade or Pull Together to stop him.
  • The Hand (portent 4): rises through the floor at night and takes what is most valued in reach. It is here for whatever is in the water that night: an offering, a bucket, the horn idol from Tor’s shrine, Beth.

Hand of Daagon

Solitary, large, emanation, terrifying, cautious, curious

HP 23; Armor 4 (resilience), 0 vs bronze
Damage many hands (hand, close, grabby, ignores armor)
Special qualities blind; tremorsense; reaches through matter like it was water
Instinct to feed the Mouth

  • Sink into the stone, or emerge from it
  • Reach through armor, clothing, flesh and bone to take what is most valued
  • Retreat with its prize, back to the water
  • Bronze in the water: spears and knives from every home. Fighting it means being in the water with it, in the dark, on glass.
  • Kill it: the village keeps the Cistern. Its hide is a piece for the Demonhide Cloak.
  • Let it go: it takes the prize down through the floor, and the floor is whole behind it.

The Stone

Stonetop Keystone Climax
“Faded runes wind the Stone from the ground to where the eye gives up. The lightning-scars are fresh. Put a hand on it and it is warm, and it is trembling, very slightly, in time with the water below.”

The seventh nail. It cannot be pulled from above. The Quern must break it from beneath, and when it comes the bluff itself is the battlefield. The runes are the binding written out; the road-stones carry the same hand.

  • Read the runes: Know Things with a justification, per Maker runes. 10+ the Stone is a nail, there are seven, and the runes name the road as the chain; 7–9 one of those, and a way to learn more (Twymor, the Otavix, the Hillfolk verse); 6− the Scepter offers to read it for you.
  • Ring the anvil (the rite): at the height of a storm, with the Cistern full, someone sings the binding-note into the water while lightning walks the Stone. The note comes from Titan Bones, Tor’s Fist, a Stone Lord dormitory, or a makerglass chime. Thunderous Bellow is that voice. Roll +CON or +WIS: 10+ the Quern sleeps where it lies and every portent clears; 7–9 it sleeps, but the singer takes the Bellow’s 1d6 twice and the Cistern floor cracks; 6− the note is wrong and the Quern comes now.
  • Peacebond the keystone: Peacebond glyphs cut around the Stone’s foot sap the will to struggle of anything that surfaces here. The Quern hesitates at the bluff, which is the round the party needs.

If the Quern surfaces

Portent 6, or a 6− on the rite. It comes up under the bluff in the storm and the switchback goes first. It cannot be killed, only hurt back down the line.

The Quern, Abaloura

Solitary, huge, primordial, terrifying, forceful

HP 30; Armor 6 (size, resilience), 2 vs orichalcum or black iron
Damage grinding bulk (reach, area, forceful, messy)
Special qualities blind; tremorsense; cannot leave the binding-line
Instinct to shrug off what pins it

  • Throw the ground
  • Swallow a building
  • Bellow so the Cistern floor cracks
  • Go still, and listen
  • What hurts it: the Otavix’s pins used as spears; the black iron nail from Underfalls if they kept it; the Stone’s own lightning if someone can bring the beast to the Stone’s foot in the storm; Nerth Serpent’s Vigor for whoever is closest. The Flaming Sword’s spirit gets its glory here.
  • 15+ damage from those sources in a scene: it withdraws down the line and the portents reset to 4. It has not gone anywhere.
  • The militia: spears and shields from every home, the way the village has always drilled, on a bluff that is coming apart. Pull Together.
  • If the Stone breaks: the bluff goes into the Cistern and the Quern lies down in the crater, asleep again. See Outcomes.

(the nine-fingered stranger, if used) “Three I killed. This one I only pinned, and I was young, and I was tired. You will not kill it either. Pin it better than I did.”

Deep Crack

Sealed Cave Lore
“Behind the plinth, a hairline split in the floor. Warm air, wet iron and spoiled fruit. Down the crack, a long way down, something breathes in, and the peacebond loops on the walls seem to tighten with it.”

The Deep Crack on the Sealed Cave sheet is a side-vent of the binding-line. The peacebonded dead were not the only thing that cave was sealed against. Run the cave from its own sheet; this is what the crack adds.

  • Listen at the crack: as the Cistern move, +WIS, with the same result. The shades answer too.
  • Study the loops with the rhyme in mind: Know Things. The peacebond is a lesser cousin of the binding-song, and a titan has a will to sap. This is where the party learns Peacebond can hold the Quern still for a round, at the Stone or on its back.
  • Open the crack: Make Pliant the Stone or a week with picks. Warm air becomes a wind. Advance a portent; the shades rise in the Gallery.

Dialog (pick 1)

(a morose shade) “We lay down because the loops asked us to. It is asking, too, down there. It does not want to lie down. It has been lying down for so long.”
(Glasbren, from the roots) “The hill has a heartbeat now. It did not, when I came. I do not like a hill with a heartbeat. Make it stop and I will tell you a secret that is worth it.”

Exits

Back through the cave and down to the village → The Cistern: “Half a day. The smell follows you as far as the sheep-runs.”

Tor’s Fist

Whitefangs Expedition
“A cleft at the crown of the peak, the walls fifty feet apart and rune-cut on both faces, and the empty shape between them is the shape of the Stone. When the storm hits, the cleft rings one note, clean, and the lights come after.”

The Stone was carved from here (Tor’s Fist). Chart a Course as the book gives it: late spring or summer, five days from the Foothills, a guide, warm clothes, a mountaineer, and the final sheer climb in wind. This is also a holy site far from the Things Below, where a cursed book can be buried.

  • Hear the note: stand in the cleft through a storm. Defy Danger +CON against the cold and the lightning. Whoever endures it can carry the binding-note home and sing it into the Cistern (the Stone).
  • Bury the Codex here: the cleft accepts it. Hec’tumel will offer, on the way up, to say how to stop the Quern in exchange for the book staying in the pack. What he says is true and it is the rite of the Cistern with a life in it.
  • Draw the Quern’s attention: a 6− on the climb and the mountain answers the note from below. The line runs here too, faintly.

Exits

Down and south, six days → The Stone: “From the last ridge you can see the Stone catching the sun, a splinter off this mountain, three days’ walk of grass away.”

Travel The chain

  • A tremor on the road: the site move. On a 6− here, the slab under someone tilts and the black water takes their pack.
  • Fellow travelers: immigrants heading up to the Delve for the wages Mutra is suddenly paying. Roll disposition from the road’s table.

Titan Bones

Hillfolk Lore
“Kiln is sitting on a rib the length of a house, not looking at you. His band is packed to move. When he finally speaks it is to the hill, not to you.”

The wayside under the skeleton (Titan Bones). Kiln’s band keeps the tale of the fourth beast, the one that was nailed and not slain, and the Barrow Builder verse whole, with the cadence the second stanza lost.

  • Ask for the tale: he gives it if the party’s debt from the raid stands, or for the news that his sons are alive in the Delve.
  • Ask for the cadence: that is a thing sung, not told, and the band’s singer is a girl of nine. Kiln asks a price in return: his sons home, without the band’s name in Mutra’s ledger.
  • Hillfolk on the pins: mining is sacrilege. Pulling a nail from Grandfather’s back is a thing there is no word for. If the band learns what the boys are doing, Kiln cannot take them back.

The Terraces

Gordin’s Delve Politics
“Every third building on the Terraces has a fresh crack in it. Mutra’s compound has none, because it is built into Maker stone. A lean man in a clerk’s coat stands at its gate with a sword too good for him, and his hands are bandaged.”

Mutra’s compound, the Swap, and the other Bosses. Mutra holds one pulled pin and is paying double wages for the crew going after five and six. Odo Thriceborn is losing tenements to every quake. The Otavix’s sect has a stall at the Swap and is quietly out of silver.

  • Ask Around: the pin crew went down the Third Delve four days ago with Gessa and two Hillfolk boys. The Delve moves for hire, with the local Prosperity.
  • Mutra: sells the pin for a Surplus of whisky and the Inn’s next year of timber, or for the party doing a thing to Odo. He does not believe in the rhyme and he does not need to.
  • Odo: gives the party a prospector who knows the Third Delve’s upper forks, if Mutra’s crew comes back short and the Terraces stop shaking. He wants to be told the story first, loudly.
  • The Otavix’s sect: three of them, formal and unhappy. They release the two pins to whoever will go down with them, and one of them will alloy at Filj’s forge or at Foundry, watched.
  • Llyr: the sword’s spirit wants glory and a Boss’s gate is not it. Commune with it, or let Llyr overhear what is under the mountain. He will ask to come. He is burning himself alive by inches and calling it purpose, and the Quern is the first purpose big enough.
  • Kiln’s sons: ask after them by description and Mutra’s people will know why. The boys are below with Gessa (Beast’s Back).

Second Delve

Gordin’s Delve Daagon
“The last landing is a ledge over black water that does not end. The miners’ brick wall is down again. There are coins on the ledge, and a shoe, and the water is moving in rings, the same rings as home.”

The subterranean lake and its cult (Delve secrets). The whisper to the Quern comes through this water. Mutra’s people bring the offerings; Mutra has never come down himself.

  • Wall it: bendis-root smoke (a sack, Value 2, from Marshedge goods) and bronze runes on the ledge, per cleansing corrupted sites. A day’s work and a Defy Danger against the whispers. Pins five and six, and the empty sockets of one to four. A pin is a rune-cast orichalcum spike the length of a man, sunk to the head. The sockets weep. This chamber is on the binding-line and every tremor starts here.

Outcomes

  • Pins seated, Stone rung: the Quern sleeps. The Cistern rings once more, clean, and stops. The Otavix cuts its sect-mark into the kerb. Kiln’s sons come home. Twymor’s stanza gets a last line, and the children skip to it.
