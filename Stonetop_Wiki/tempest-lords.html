Tempest Lords

They were, perhaps, the first of the Makers. They charted the seas and the stars. They leashed the elements to their will. They built mighty ships and flying castles, sailed forth from their island home and conquered. All who stood against them were reduced to ash and glass and slag.

The pride of the Tempest Lords was warranted, but their hubris led to their fall. They sought to tear their entire homeland free from the earth, to set it floating above the world they ruled.

They failed. Calamity followed.

The seas boiled and belched forth horrors.

Fire rained from the sky. Their island capital sank beneath the waves, and the spine of their empire was shattered. Civil war erupted. Colonies rebelled.

The Tempest Lords who survived were scattered to the winds. Some tried to restore the empire, but met violent ends at the hands of rivals or would-be subjects.

Soon, only a few Tempest Lords remained, enigmas even among other Makers.

One of these survivors, Stormcatcher, made her way to the the Flats, beyond even the most far-flung of the empire's old outposts. There, between four other nations of Makers, she built her tower. And when the horrors of the Things Below were set loose upon the world, Stormcatcher's tower—what folks now call the Ruined Tower—was the last of the Makers' enclaves to fall.

Language and names

Tempest Lord writing was ideogrammatic (characters represent ideas, not sounds), and they spoke a primordial language—all who heard it understood it as their native tongue. As such, the names of Tempest

Lords are rendered as descriptions, titles, or honorifics.

Example names: Brinedrinker, Eyes-Like-Lightning, Fardweller, Foehammer, Lightning-Is-Her-Spear, His-Smile-Isa-Harbor, Mind-Like-Fire, Moonspeaker, Oarbreaker, Rainchaser, Soother-Of-Waves, Sparkspitter, Squalltalker, Starfinder, Taller-Than-Masts, Voice-of-Thunder, Wavecutter, Waymaker, Windwalker

When other Makers spoke or wrote of them, they used their own tongue. Windwalker was called Vetrodokha by the Green Lords and Avaelkerzhaer by the Stone Lords.

Scars of unchecked power

For example:

  • A crater, lousy with fulgurites and shards of glass
  • A fractal, branching pattern etched in a bare expanse of stone
  • A rune-etched menhir or large hunk of makerglass, near which some fundamental law or force (time, gravity, space, light, etc.) is warped
  • A barren stretch of glass pebbles, strangely colored and always warm
  • The vitrified remains of a Maker, a monster, a small army, etc. Crude glass shapes hinting at what they once were, flash-frozen in time Places scarred by such power might very well be radioactive, haunted sites, and/or corrupted by the Things Below.

Fragments of a ship or sky-island

For example:

  • A ship's hull, mostly buried but somehow still intact
  • A torn sail of aetherium mesh, strapped to a rusty metal cross
  • Strange objects, strewn about and long-buried: a crumpled brass cylinder, an etched metal disk, an oversized ship's wheel, a rudder, etc.
  • A rocky outcrop, the stone alien to the surrounding terrain, tilted at an odd angle
  • A cracked slab of granite, big as the granary and shot through with aetherium, floating a few feet above the ground and spinning listlessly
  • Slabs of worked stone: broken, buried, and scattered, like a child's toy dashed from impossible heights

Enigmatic structures

For example:

  • Iron spires, like hollow rusted trees, placed in a line across the landscape
  • A black cube the size of a house; when lightning strikes, the earth lights up for miles with glowing lines that fade over the next few minutes
  • Stone plinths along a ridge line, some of them topped with 6-foot-tall sculptures, like tulips, spinning in the breeze
  • Copper cylinders, each maybe 8 feet tall and caked in verdigris; mostly buried in white and chalky soil
  • Megaliths, etched with ideographs that describe the motions of the stars
  • A bronze plaque upon a stone slab, the only notable feature for miles; untouched by lichen; deeply etched ideographs begin with the phrase, "This is not a place of honor"

0) and the appropriate chemics (Value 0) into an ingot or casting of pure aethe-◇ rium (magical, crude, possibly dangerous,

Aetherium Urn

◇, magical, dangerous

The lid is held fast by no apparent means.

It takes some effort to pry open and eagerly snaps back into place.

When the urn is open and empty, it draws raw elemental force into it like a lodestone draws iron. With the lid in place, the force is safely stored. If the urn is damaged or dented (not hard), any stored force escapes, violently.

Bronze Ring

◇, magical, fireproof, hours About 8 inches in diameter, an inch thick throughout, scorched and smeared with ash. A button protrudes, with a Tempest Lord glyph that might mean "stubborn."

When you find the ring, it has enough power to run for hours.

When you press the button, the ring fixes in place, defying gravity. It simply will not budge. The effect ends if the ring is bent or broken, or when it runs out of power.

There's no obvious way to end the effect early. When the ring runs out of power, the button pops out and the effect ends.

When you heat the ring in a forge or kiln for about eight hours, it gains an hour of use (up to 3 hours).

Floating Sash

magical

A length of bluish-white lamé, maybe 6 inches wide by 6 feet long, snaking about in the air. Like a belt, but for someone far larger than you.

When you wrap the sash securely around your waist, it buoys you against falls—you never fall faster than if you had fallen your own height.

Silvery Tuning Fork

magical, loud

When you strike this tuning fork against a hard surface, it emits a loud tone for about a minute. Nearby (reach, area) magical effects and creatures become visible: shaking, vibrating, distorted. The noise draws the attention of any spirits or magically attuned beings for miles around.

Storm-Cloud Potion

magical, fragile

A glass vial of dark-grey liquid, roiling and shot through with sparks.

When you drink a storm-cloud potion, lightning courses through you. Take damage (ignores armor) and mark a debility. Should you survive, hold 3 Storm.

While you hold any Storm, you cannot feel pain or fear, tell friend from foe, speak coherently, or exercise patience or care. Add your Storm to your Armor, and your melee and thrown attacks do +Storm damage (+loud, +forceful).

When you reduce someone or something to 0 HP, lose 1 Storm.

Dangers

Monsters and entities

  • A thunder drake or luglfsk, drawn to aetherium
  • Ghosts or revenants, victims of the Tempest Lord's experiments, callousness, or wrath
  • An archon, ensuring some Tempest Lord artifact goes unused
  • An elemental spirit or a fundamental, tethered to a device or artifice
  • A primordial entity or something corrupted by the Things Below, vitrified and held in stasis.

Radiation

Sometimes, the Tempest Lords unleashed such terrible power that it poisoned all it touched: soil, bone, stone, metal. Poisoned them all so deeply that now, still, centuries and centuries later, those things poison all that draws near.

Such poisonous places are often corrupted by the Things Below. But even when they are not, they are dangerous.

  • Point out flora or fauna, stunted and strange
  • Make their skin itch, make them feel unwell
  • Inflict a debility that refuses to go away
  • Have symptoms appear (or return) hours or days after exposure
  • Have symptoms linger or worsen, perhaps for months

PCs or NPCs exposed to such radiation are prone to developing terrible, wasting illness, months or years later. Write this up as a threat (an affliction).

Vitrification

A few Tempest Lords could call up a bolt of power so raw and potent that it would turn whatever it touched into glass.

It was dangerous for even them to use, but no foe could stand against it.

It's possible that some device still exists that can unleash this sort of power. If so, treat it as a threat (a MacGuffin; Instinct to tempt people into using it). It would

require vast amounts of energy and involve extraordinary risks.

When you are struck by a vitrifying bolt, reduce your HP to 0 and roll +CON: on a 10+, you merely suffer terrible burns and will soon be at Death's Door; on a 7-9, you and your possessions are turned to jagged glass, but you might be restored; on a 6-, you die but your corpse, your soul, and your possessions are also turned into jagged glass—should the vitrification be reversed, it's as though you rolled a 6- on Death's Door.

Vitrification can be reversed, but you must decide how it's done. Perhaps it involves:

  • vast amounts of power and great risk;
  • a sympathetic link to the victim, or whatever vitrified them in the first place;
  • the intervention of a powerful god or primordial entity;
  • spilling the life-blood of your kin upon the jagged glass;
  • a spell of transference, where someone else becomes vitrified instead;
  • a spiritwalker releasing the vitrified soul, and guiding it to a suitable vessel;
  • something different for each victim, or for each source of vitrification;
  • some combination of the above; or
  • something else entirely.

Elemental Vortex

Picture a filigree sphere of aetherium, maybe 6 inches across, a glowing mote swirling at its center. This device—called a matrix—binds an elemental spirit and absorbs a particular force, with which the spirit is able to manifest a swirling vortex.

A spirit bound to a matrix must follow any orders given in the Tempest Lord's primordial language. As few alive can give them such orders, any matrices encountered will likely be acting on orders from centuries ago.

Power and size: Each matrix absorbs a particular type of power, and current HP of its vortex reflects its current power.

As a vortex gains or loses HP, its size, armor, and damage change as well. At 0 HP, it dissipates and its matrix falls to the ground, inert until it absorbs more HP.

Attacking the matrix: A matrix itself has only 3 HP. If reduced to 0 HP, the spirit within escapes, dealing the vortex's current damage (+area, one range tag greater than normal).

When a PC tries to strike the matrix inside an active vortex, the vortex's Armor applies. Also, tell them the requirements and/ or consequences. such as:

  • You'll have disadvantage to Clash/ Let Fly/deal damage
  • You'll need to create or wait for an opening, where the matrix is exposed
  • You'll need to get in close/let it engulf you
  • You'll need a longer/heavier/magical weapon
  • You'll risk losing/ruining your weapon
  • You'll suffer its attack, no matter what

Pick options based on the nature of the vortex, the PC's weapon, and the rest of their fictional positioning. The larger the vortex, the harder it is to strike the matrix.

Damage smash (damage and tags by HP)
Special qualities half damage from stabbing or slashing weapons (unless magical)
Instinct to topple and undermine

  • Manifest a churning mass out of nearby earth, sand, and rocks

amorphous, grumpy, clumsy

HP varies; Armor by HP, +1 (made of rock)

• Pin a smaller foe (+grabby)

• Grind a pinned foe, dealing damage w/advantage (+messy)

• Ruin a sturdy object or structure (costs HP)

An earth matrix absorbs power from pressure, blunt force, and seismic activity.

It gains HP from a week buried under earth or stone, from being at the bottom of a rockslide or cave-in, or from touching the ground during a major earthquake (not one it created).

When you strike an earth matrix with a bludgeoning attack, it takes no damage. If the blow was forceful and its vortex has 10 HP or less, it gains 1 HP. (The vortex itself has no such immunities, smash away!)

A water matrix absorbs power from currents, tides, waves, and falling water. It gains HP from a few days in a flowing stream, a few hours in a rushing river, or a few minutes in rapids or a major waterfall.

When you are engulfed by a water vortex, hold Breath. When you make a move while engulfed, and that move involves a roll, spend 1 Breath or mark a debility after rolling. If you can't do either, you drown and are at Death's Door.
