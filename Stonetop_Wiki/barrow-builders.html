Barrow Builders

When the servants of the Stone Lords rebelled, slaying their masters and unleashing the Things Below, those who died in the uprising were hailed as martyrs. The survivors raised mounds of earth and stone to honor them, simple barrows built with human hands and human arts.

Society collapsed. Horror swept the land.

Petty tyrants and sorcerer-kings rose to power. They had barrows built to honor loyal vassals or to seal away the ghosts of those they had betrayed.

Fleeting kingdoms rose and fell. Rulers raised grand barrows and had themselves interred with riches, slaves, and followers in tow. Fueled by dark magic and the power of the Things Below, some of these tyrants reigned on even after death.

In time, the power of the Things Below diminished. Subjects rose up and overthrew their masters. Tyrants began to fall.

Those who could not be killed were sealed away in barrows. The current age arrived.

Many barrows still remain. A few are simple graves. Many are haunted or cursed.

Some imprison ancient evil. They dot the World's End, especially in the Steplands. The Hillfolk shun them, and warn all travelers away. For they descended from the Barrow Builders, and those who cast them off. They know what horrors those earthen mounds contain.

Names

Breton- or Cornish-inspired, similar to Hillfolk names but with more vowels: Alana, Arzhel, Brandan, Corent, Deniel, Enora, Gwenael, Herve, Judikael, Karenza, Katell, Maben, Margh, Nolwenn, Piran, Ronan, Winoc, Yanick, Yezekael.

Hooks

  • An artifact that the PCs want lies in a haunted barrow mound
  • Some fool unseals a barrow and lets phantom riders loose
  • Someone acquires the tether of a deathless one
  • Hillfolk are furious that one of the barrows was looted

Lore

Everyone knows:

  • That old barrows dot the region
  • That they're smaller and cruder than Maker-ruins
  • That Hillfolk shun them
  • That they supposedly contain great treasure and great danger

One might know:

  • Where a few barrows can be found
  • That they were built by the Hillfolk's (reviled) ancestors
  • Of a particularly (in)famous figure among them
  • The horrors from those times
  • A tale about a specific barrow

Very few know:

  • What a given barrow contains
  • Where a specific figure is buried
  • The true story behind a barrow

Sites

Barrow Builder sites are most common in the Steplands, especially around Blackwater Lake and Three Coven Lake. Plenty can also be found in the Flats, the South Manmarch, and along the Dread River. A few can be found in the Huffel Peaks and as far north as the Foothills.

Artifacts

The Barrow Builders gladly looted and repurposed whatever treasures they could, and often dabbled with dangerous powers. Their sites often contain artifacts related to:

  • Death and the undying
  • The Things Below
  • The Stone Lords
  • Other Makers, especially the Forge Lords or the Tempest Lords

Various Treasures

  • A rusty Y-shaped pendant (Value 0), symbol of the cult who fed the Voice of the Eternal Maw
  • A bronze blade from a metal construct's limb, remade into a brutal axe (close, messy, awkward, +1 damage, Value 1)
  • A map (fragile) to the barrow of a famously rich warlord
  • Ancient grains in wax-sealed jugs, still good and worth 1 Surplus (immobile)
  • A Maker-era gold coin (Value 2), found in a mass grave, the tether for a ghost
  • A fine torc of aetherium around a skeleton's neck; shocks its wearer if they try to remove it or engage in any sort of violence
  • A spear (◇ close, thrown, beautiful, indestructible, 2 piercing, Value 3) made of makerglass, shaft and all, found in a hero's tomb
  • A ◇◇ Stone Lord's cleft skull, coated in gold and studded with gems (cumbersome, Value 4)

An Old Bronze Dagger

hand, magical

Tinged green, but sharp and sturdy. In the chaotic days after the Makers fell, it was the weapon of a desperate father. He killed his own brother with it, to protect his children's food. He killed others, too, to make sure his family had enough. So many, many others.

The dagger holds a shard of that father's desperate will. When you Seek Insight about someone while carrying the dagger, add the following to the list of questions you can ask. If your hand is on the hilt, you can ask one of these questions for free, even on a 6-.

  • What of mine do they most want?
  • How am I most vulnerable to them?
  • What do they carry that is useful to me?

If a PC keeps the dagger, treat it as a threat (a MacGuffin; Instinct to cause paranoia).

The Three-star Crown

◇ magical, beautiful, Value 4

A circlet of whitest gold, set with three black sapphires that shine like stars. It was made by a renowned smith, an apprentice of the Forge Lords, at the command of a petty warlord. The warlord was so pleased, he maimed the smith's hands so that she could never make its rival. The smith killed herself in despair, cursing the crown to steal the hopes of all who wore it.

When you wear the crown and interact with someone who fears or respects you, you roll +CHA with advantage. Alas, each time you don the crown, roll +WIS: on a 10+, tell the GM one of your hopes and why you doubt it can be achieved; on a 7-9, tell them a hope you have abandoned; on a 6-, you fall into a deep despair that lasts until you rouse yourself to face a mortal danger.

If the crown is destroyed—the gems shattered, the metal reduced to slag—then the despair is lifted and all lost hopes are restored.

A Lead Bracelet

magical, Value 1

This heavy, black metal torc is set with a red gem. It feels good upon your wrist.

Makes you feel important.

When you wear the torc and threaten someone that you consider beneath you, lose HP equal to the torc's current Cost ( to start). They are filled with fear; the craven will flee, cower, or grovel, and even the brave will flinch and hesitate. Each time you use the torc, the Cost increases by 1 die size (max ).

When you wear the torc for a week without anyone questioning your authority, decrease the Cost by one step (min ).

Banefire

Curtains or pillars of heatless flame, blue or green or purple, with no obvious source of fuel. Banefire features in many tales of the barrows: to repel, to imprison, or just as an uncanny feature of the mounds.

  • Spring forth with little/no warning
  • Blind them with sudden light
  • Block a path or a line of sight
  • Burn spirits, the undead, and living souls ( damage, ignores armor)

Something interesting: Ghosts and other spirits fear banefire as much as mortals, but the inhabitants of some barrows can control it.

Something useful: Banefire burns only where it's meant to; it never spreads. Each banefire is anchored to a blackened bone, all that remains of the mortal whose body and soul were sacrificed to fuel the flames.

Barrow Wight

Group, undead, corrupted, terrifying, hardy, hoarder

HP 14; Armor 4 (resilience), 2 vs. bronze or fire (mail)
Damage rusty sword (close, forceful) or crushing grip (hand, grabby, forceful)
Special qualities bound to their barrow
Instinct to protect their hoard

  • Utter an echoing warning

As you might expect, grave robbing was always a problem for the Barrow Builders.

But a clever sorcerer had a solution! So many hateful spirits roamed the world in those days. Why not bind some to the corpses of the deceased, and let the dead guard their own treasure?

Phantom Rider

Group, large, spirit, undead, organized

HP 14; Armor 2 (lacks organs, shield)
Damage spear (close, thrown) or trample (hand, forceful) or bow (near)
Special qualities bound to their bones; can travel far and wide; can't manifest in daylight; takes + damage from silver
Instinct to seek tribute, to lord over others

  • Speed across the landscape like the wind itself
  • Form a body of soil, dust, and mist
  • Flank and harry their foes

For a time, it was common for warriors to pledge both their lives and their deaths to their lieges, and to be interred with their horses and their fellows in their master's tomb. Thus, they could live on in death, still basking in a grim and joyless glory.

Deathless Ones

In the time of the Barrow Builders, many warlords and sorcerers were obsessed with immortality. A number of them found it—or something like it—through undeath.

To create a deathless one, imagine a warlord (someone similar to a Manmarcher chief, North Manmarch), a sorcerer, a necromancer, or a hdour. Consider making them corrupted by the Things Below. Add the undead tag, and give them +4 HP because they are animated by more than biology.

From there, pick or roll for the tether that sustains them, how it sustains them, and its limitation. Then pick or roll for the deathless one's current state. Consider where and how they (or their influence) might be encountered. Let this all inform their story, their personality, and their stat block.
