The Whitefang Mountains

The Whitefang Mountains form the northern border of the World's End, running from the Huffel Peaks in the northwest, all along the Great Wood and the North Manmarch, to the Dread River and beyond in the east. Beyond them lie nothing but the Frozen Wastes.

The mountains are tall and jagged, steep and forbidding. Yet they hide almost indescribable beauty: waterfalls, deep pine forests, sweeping meadows. They also hide a surprising number of settlements. Barrier Pass is closest to Stonetop, but many more lie beyond.

Impressions

Always

  • Snow-covered peaks, like mighty teeth
  • Wind: howling, whispering, gusting
  • Monkeys screeching and chittering in the valleys, leaping through the trees
  • Prayer-flags fluttering in the wind
  • The elation of reaching a summit; the despair of realizing it was just a ridge, and you're only half-way

Spring

  • Slopes and vales bright with rhododendron: purple, red, and orange
  • Trickle and rush of snowmelt

Hooks

  • Storms around Tor's Fist grow stronger; the colors shift to red and yellow
  • A band of kyakaara slips out of the mountains and is eyeing Stonetop
  • A southern merchant is intent on finding a mountain village, to trade for ivory and fine wool
  • Mountain apes hold an artifact/scrap of lore in their hoard
  • An apprentice shantyara needs help retrieving her dead master's sword from the bhoka who killed him

Summer

  • Lush green below; stern, colorless peaks above
  • Birdsong echoing and echoing

Autumn

  • Meadows turned brown and red and gold
  • Mist filling the valleys, clouds gliding by above

Winter

  • Snow cloaking the valleys, choking passes
  • Tears freezing, sweat soaking you under warm clothes

Tor's Fist

Almost straight north of Stonetop, this lonely mountain juts above its neighbors.

It's not the tallest peak in the Whitefangs, but it's tall enough. When summer storms roll up from the south and crash against the mountains, the worst of them swirl around Tor's Fist. They'll lash the peak with lightning, and in the aftermath, green and purple lights dance around the crown.

What's up there?

Clearly Tor's Fist holds some mystery, but its nature is for you to decide. Perhaps the mountain's peak holds…

  • the tomb of Stormcatcher, or her son, or her lover;
  • a cleft, from which the Stone itself was carved;
  • one of the last thirteen Rime Lords, still in stasis, protected by powerful tulpas;
  • a potent storm-spirit, corrupted by the Things Below, but bound there by ancient Rime Lord magic;
  • the domain of a powerful, temperamental Fae;
  • a shattered portal, torn open and ruined;
  • a people of great stature, who worship Tor;
  • the last of the Tempest Lords, grumpy and aloof;
  • some combination of the above; or
  • something else entirely.

Mountain folk

The Whitefangs host a surprising number of settlements. Most are small farming valleys, even more isolated than Stonetop. But there are also a number of fortress-monasteries, like Barrier Pass.

Something interesting: The mountain folk's ancestors were disciples of the Rime Lords, and Rime Lord teachings still influence the culture. But time, isolation, and competing philosophies have led to vastly different local traditions. An idea or practice that is lauded in one village might be ridiculed in the next. And in the village beyond that? It might get you killed.

Something useful: Communities occasionally trade with their nearest neighbors, and folk in one settlement are familiar with the ways of the next—at least well enough to avoid offense or violence. But if you seek a guide or informant, find one who's actually been there, lest you have your ears filled with exaggerated nonsense.

Names

Nepalese- and Tibetan-inspired: Aandha, Araayin, Bisheej, Choden, Dawa, Dhaar, Dorji, Duga, Fayurma, Hopan, Jamya, Jyat, Keshhay, Ksali, Kundari, Kunza, Lhamo, Mitis, Nobu, Nyado, Passan, Pratar, Puyari, Radhaa, Ravar, Sajadr, Sonam, Supar, Tani, Tashi, Tenzi, Tseri, Wanchu, Yonta.

Questions

  • Who among you have traveled here before? When and why?
  • Who do you know (in Gordin's Delve, perhaps) who hails from the Whitefangs?
  • Why do you think storms swirl around Tor's Fist? What do you think those strange lights are?
  • Did you once live in a farm valley, or a fortress-monastery?
  • What drove you to leave?
  • What behavior in Stonetop is strictly taboo where you once lived?
  • What practice is common where you once lived, but utterly baffling to folk in Stonetop?
  • How is (are) the language(s) of the mountain folk different from those of Stonetop?
  • Do they worship ___ up there? If so, how is that worship different than in Stonetop?

Farm valleys

The valleys of the Whitefangs can be surprisingly pleasant. Streams flow almost year-round, freezing over but still running throughout winter. Below the tree line, tall skinny conifers grow in abundance. Many of these vales hold steadings.

Fortress-monasteries

At least a dozen fortress-monasteries remain, scattered throughout the Whitefang Mountains. Barrier Pass is one of the largest and most powerful. Each is dedicated to preserving the teachings of the Rime Lords—or at least, preserving their version of them!

Size hamlet (~25 to 75 souls)

Population +0

Prosperity +0

  • Trade with nearby farm valleys (food, livestock, wool)

Resources

  • Tithes from nearby farm valleys
  • Terraced gardens (barley, lentils)
  • Livestock (goats, sheep, milk, meat)
  • Fine wool
  • Parchment, vellum, ink
  • Trades: bronze-smithing, weaving, dyeing, tanning, scribing, etc.
  • Rime Lord artifacts

Defenses +2

  • Guardians
  • At least one adept
  • One or more tulpas
  • Weapons/armor of bronze, maybe dark ice
  • Defensible position

A typical fortress-monastery has 25-50 residents, split between monks and guardians, plus a handful of layfolk. Most receive food and material support from a nearby farm valley or two.

In turn, guardians patrol the region and monks (ostensibly) guide, teach, and serve as an example for villagers.

Dangers

Hazards

  • Getting lost (maze of mountains, canyons, passes, and valleys)
  • Sheer rock face to climb, and a reason to climb it
  • Steep slopes, unstable footing (narrow paths, wet stone, scree, thin waterlogged soil, mud, crumbling rock)
  • Flash flooding in valleys/on slopes
  • An unexpected obstacle/hazard: go forward and risk it, or backtrack and lose half a day?
  • Mudslides and rockslides

Harsh mountain weather

  • Baking sun, dry air
  • Fog banks/low clouds, blocking vision and soaking everything
  • Wind: drowning out voices, threatening to toss you aside
  • Rain and wind rolling up from below
  • Rain and sleet: pelting you, soaking you, threatening to wash you away
  • Thunder or lightning: making you flinch/jerk/jolt, triggering rockslides
  • Snow: hiding a drop, burying a pass, making trails impassible
  • Brutal cold, leading to hypothermia & frostbite (next page)

Avalanche

Instinct to gather mass, to bury all in its path

  • Have one cascade down a distant slope, majestic and terrifying

Everyone who lives in these mountains learns to spot the signs.

• Point out the snowpack, looming on the slopes above

• Note how a storm adds mass to the snowpack

• Point out the dimples as sun/warmth undermine its structure

When an avalanche starts above them, start the following:

Hypothermia & Frostbite

It gets cold in the mountains. Very, very cold.

  • Start them shivering, despite fire/ warm clothing
  • Test their endurance on even simple tasks
  • Have them lose their footing/grip
  • Inflict a debility
  • Have frostbite set in ( damage, ignores armor)
  • Have frostbite get worse (+messy)
  • Compel them to do something foolish (go to sleep, shed clothing, etc.)

Altitude Sickness

Dangerously thin air starts a couple thousand feet below the snowline, and gets worse the higher you go. Locals are accustomed to the thin air, but even they must be careful.

  • Test their endurance to keep going, or just to breathe
  • Inflict a debility
  • Reveal that they've missed something
  • Have them collapse, unable to continue
  • Fill their lungs with fluid, swell their brains, have them roll for Death's Door

Something interesting: Disciples of the Rime Lords practice techniques to thrive in the thin air, but they take months or years to master.

Something useful: One can acclimate safely by ascending no more than ~1,000 feet each day, and even then, extreme exertion can be dangerous. A tea steeped from purple drakewort can alleviate symptoms, but an overdose can cause death by suffocation.

Monsters

  • Predators: cave bears, cougars, and wolves
  • Spirits of the wild
  • Wraiths of those who died of starvation and want, or corrupted by the Howling Curse
  • Tulpas, free-roaming or bound to something nearby

People

  • Guardians, monks, or novices from Barrier Pass
  • An adept
  • A false adept, or their minions

Bhoka

Solitary, large, cautious, magical, corrupted

HP 16; Armor 1 (hide)
Damage talons (hand, close, grabby, forceful)
Special qualities blinded by full daylight or holy light, takes + damage from bronze
Instinct see below

  • Slink through trees and shadows
  • These hulking brutes dwell in dark places: caves, north-facing slopes, and deep mountain forests. They're hairy things, hunched and wild, only vaguely looking like people.

They'd be little more than dangerous beasts, easily avoided, if they weren't driven by an obsessive vice—and if they didn't wield the wild elements themselves.

Pick or roll for the bhoka's instinct and the associated moves.

Something useful: The monks claim that bhoka descended from Rime Lords who were corrupted by the Things Below. They cannot abide the sound of the Rime Lords' meditative chants, recoiling and fleeing from anyone who performs such a chant without faltering.

Like cranes, long-legged and long-necked, with long whipping tails. They're hardly a threat in warmer months. Wander into their midst, and you'll get coated in a clinging, frozen mist that often leads to hypothermia & frostbite.

Ice drake fat and blood never freezes.

The Howling Curse

Threat (affliction)

Instinct to make people into monsters

In the Time of Cataclysm, the voice of Y'aaw'kara plunged the north into madness. Whole villages turned against each other. The survivors were reduced to marauding beasts. The Rime Lords sacrificed themselves to contain the Howling Curse, but it lingers in places where cruelty and violence have caused great suffering.

Places tainted by the Howling Curse are corrupted sites, hungry places or worse, full of whispers and visions that push people towards bloodshed, cruelty, and savagery. People tainted by the Howling Curse carry such whispers and visions with them.

When you give in to the bloodlust of the Howling Curse, pick up to 3 options that are not yet permanent:

When the violence comes to an end, roll +1 for each option you have chosen. If you wrought great carnage, roll with advantage. On a 10+, the options you chose are permanent, and you are now tainted by the Howling Curse (if you weren't already); on a 7-9, the options you chose linger until you vent your rage on a person or thing of your choice; on a 6-, don't mark XP, and both the rage and the options you chose fade as you catch your breath—only nightmares will remain.

When all five options above are permanent, you become a kyakaara (next page) and cease to be a PC. Tell the GM to write you up as a monster and as a threat.

During the Time of Cataclysm, kyakaara roamed freely; now they are thankfully rare. Treat them as a threat, beasts, with the following grim portents and impending doom.
